In brief

Phenotype: humoral deficiency treated with immunoglobulin replacement, elevated αβ DNTs without an identified classical ALPS-associated variant (FAS, FASLG, CASP8, CASP10, NRAS/KRAS), and a cutaneous/digestive inflammatory background (generalized lichen planus, probable cutaneous Crohn’s disease) consistent with IFN-I/IFN-γ activity.

Central question: could a heterozygous YBX1 variant (c.231−96C>G), for which several convergent tools favor partial splice-related hypomorphism, link this humoral deficiency and inflammatory background through a single molecular pathway, rather than invoking independent mechanisms for each abnormality?

Why now: a study published in August 2026 shows, for the first time, that YBX1 loss is sufficient to trigger an IL‑6/STAT3 response and increased interferon-stimulated genes through Pol III RNAs and RIG‑I — an experimental mechanism that could link humoral deficiency and inflammation.

Full details, sources and explicit limitations below — a falsifiable research hypothesis, not an established diagnosis.

2 · Central node: partial splice-related YBX1 hypomorphism

candidate molecular basis
YBX1 c.231−96C>G · heterozygous

The VUS (chr1:42693394 C>G; AD 4/8; DP 12; GQ 99) receives two concordant deep-learning predictions for exactly the same cryptic donor at −5: SpliceAI DS_DG = 0.439 and Pangolin DS_SG = 0.106, without a substantial acceptor gain. MaxEntScan confirms that the alternative allele strongly strengthens the local motif at the same site, with a score increasing from 4.11 to 9.57. This convergence supports donor gain but does not demonstrate its use in RNA.

hierarchical interpretation of the predictions
very limited support
Global gain of function

The models predict donor-site gain without determining the direction of the protein’s functional effect. A global GoF is not supported by these predictions; it cannot be excluded on this basis alone.

primary molecular interpretation
Partial splice-related hypomorphism

Creation of the cryptic donor would be expected to compete with normal splicing. A pseudoexon, aberrant junction or NMD would therefore make a partial reduction in normal transcript/protein more likely than a global GoF.

exploratory alternative
Separation of functions

A stable isoform could theoretically uncouple certain outputs. This hypothesis remains secondary to the more direct model of partial hypomorphism, supported by convergent splicing predictions.

Main hypothesis

Partial YBX1 hypomorphism: a possible reduction in normal transcript/protein, potentially impairing BCR signaling and Ig translation. This is the priority working hypothesis for the humoral deficiency, to be tested at the RNA level and in the patient’s cells. Insufficient control of endogenous RNAs could also promote inflammatory dysregulation (IL‑6/STAT3, ISGs; section 3). Global GoF is not supported; separation of functions remains an exploratory alternative.

newly documented direct link
YBX1 loss → Pol III RNAs–RIG-I → IL‑6/STAT3 + ISGs ↑

In cellular models, YBX1 loss is sufficient to spontaneously trigger an IL‑6/STAT3 response and increased interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs). Inhibition of RNA polymerase III transcription or RIG‑I signaling abolishes this response: YBX1 may therefore normally help mask or control immunogenic endogenous RNAs.

Relevance to the patient: this finding provides a direct mechanistic bridge between YBX1 hypomorphism and the inflammatory/interferon component, potentially converging with IFIH1–IRF5. However, it demonstrates neither the variant’s effect nor its reproduction in the patient’s cells.

observation and hypothesis
αβ DNTs ↑: interpretation against ALPS thresholds

Total DNTs: 140/mm³ (7.6% of T cells), including αβ⁺ 47/mm³ and γδ⁺ 93/mm³. αβ DNTs account for ≈2.56% of T cells (47/1,837) and ≈1.93% of total lymphocytes (47/2,435).

ALPS consensus immunophenotypic thresholds: CD3⁺TCRαβ⁺CD4⁻CD8⁻ DNTs ≥2.5% of T cells or ≥1.5% of total lymphocytes, with a normal or elevated lymphocyte count. The αβ fraction exceeds these thresholds; this isolated criterion is insufficient to diagnose ALPS and does not demonstrate defective apoptosis or a causal link to YBX1.

These thresholds exclude γδ DNTs. The γδ majority (66.2% of DNTs) therefore does not negate the αβ elevation. Total γδ T cells are 103/mm³, or 4.2% of total lymphocytes (laboratory reference ranges: 25–200/mm³ and 1–11%).

γδ T cells are involved in lichen planus lesions, but studies do not allow these DNTs to be attributed to lichen planus or establish causality.

Exploratory extension — DNTs: CD8→DN conversion through CREMα, followed by persistence potentially promoted by the inflammatory context (section 3). The absence of an identified germline ALPS-associated variant does not validate this model by elimination. A somatic FAS abnormality restricted to a subpopulation remains a separate possibility that may escape standard germline analysis.

5 · Research question and validation pathway

Central question

Does YBX1 c.231−96C>G alter splicing in immune cells and produce partial hypomorphism capable of both reducing BCR signaling and Ig translation/production, and promoting a spontaneous IL‑6/STAT3 response with ISGs ↑ through impaired control of Pol III RNAs–RIG‑I, against a sensitized IFIH1–IRF5 background?

IFN level

IFN score before SCIG: assess whether an IFN-I background is present.

→
YBX1 RNA level

Targeted RT-PCR/RNA-seq, allele-specific quantification and assessment of cryptic junctions.

→
Priority functional level

B-cell priority: test whether B cells respond less effectively to their receptor (BCR) and produce fewer immunoglobulins.

Then DNTs: clarify their origin and investigate prolonged survival (CREMα, clonality, apoptosis assays; somatic FAS if indicated). Their elevation alone does not prove defective apoptosis.

Patient’s hypothesis

Mechanistic convergence model: IFIH1 A946T provides an IFN-I drive, IRF5 4×CGGGG amplifies TLR/cytokine signals, and SpliceAI, Pangolin and local motif strengthening by MaxEntScan favor partial splice-related hypomorphism for YBX1 c.231−96C>G over a global GoF. This hypomorphism could combine two consequences: reduced BCR signaling and Ig translation/production contributing to hypogammaglobulinemia; and, as the main inflammatory component, an IL‑6/STAT3 response and ISGs ↑ through impaired control of Pol III RNAs/RIG‑I. This response could strengthen IFIH1–IRF5 convergence and sustain T-cell activation. Tolerated homozygous GBP3 LoF could add downstream effector inefficiency and indirectly prolong the stimulus. Exploratory extensions: reduced induction of CD8 T-cell exhaustion under hypomorphism, extrapolated from a murine tumor model (section 2), and a possible link to αβ DNTs: chronic activation could promote CD8→DN conversion through CREMα; pro-survival signals (IL‑6/STAT3–cFLIP or IL‑17, without ranking these possibilities) could contribute to their persistence. Neither the IFN-I→CREMα link, reduced apoptosis nor these cytokine pathways have been demonstrated in the patient. This indirect mechanism could coexist with hypomorphism without requiring separation of functions. The familial TGF-β background remains distinct but potentially modifying; MBL/AP50 abnormalities may reflect a baseline deficiency and/or intermittent partial consumption, without a demonstrated causal link to this overall model.

Limitations to state upfront
  • Hypomorphism prioritized: the in silico audit synthesis clearly favors partial splice-related hypomorphism: SpliceAI DS_DG 0.439 and Pangolin DS_SG 0.106 target the same cryptic donor at −5, reinforced by MaxEntScan 4.11 → 9.57. However, this interpretation quantifies neither site usage nor loss of activity; the RNA/protein effect remains to be established.
  • Alternative to distinguish: separation of functions requires dissociated functional effects beyond differences in cellular sensitivity. It follows neither from the contrast between humoral deficiency and DNTs nor from an isolated reduction in T-cell apoptosis, and must not become an automatic explanation for every discordant result. Preserved splicing and function under relevant conditions would weaken the YBX1 hypothesis.
  • IFIH1 A946T and IRF5 4×CGGGG are common risk alleles; their epistasis with YBX1 is not established, and IRF5 phase remains to be confirmed.
  • The homozygous GBP3 deletion is relatively common and has no attributed Mendelian disease: it therefore cannot explain the phenotype on its own. At most, it could modulate certain antiviral defenses downstream of IFN; the idea that reduced clearance secondarily prolongs the IFN/TLR stimulus remains speculative, and the direction of its overall effect is undetermined.
  • DNTs are predominantly γδ, but the αβ fraction exceeds the ALPS consensus immunophenotypic thresholds. This result alone does not diagnose ALPS; its relationship to apoptosis, YBX1 or inflammation remains unproven.
  • Lichen planus is a cutaneous clue, not a systemic IFN measurement; the familial TGF-β background remains inferred, without an identified genetic cause.
  • The DNT “conversion followed by persistence” model relies on analogies: CREMα in lupus, synergy with complete Fas loss in mice, and STAT3–MCL‑1 in clonal T-LGLs. These findings demonstrate neither a Fas defect nor reduced apoptosis in the patient; DNT clonality is not established here.
  • The model is a falsifiable research hypothesis, not a molecular diagnosis.
